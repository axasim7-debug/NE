    1. Amazon data requested versus produced, form of data produced, and a forensic examination of
       the available produced data in this case.
           o A forensic analysis of whether the dataset is complete, unaltered, and in a format suitable
               for forensic examination.
           o Identification of gaps in data provided by the State and how missing elements impact
               forensic accuracy.
           o Evaluation of metadata, timestamps, and tracking information within the Amazon data to
               assess reliability and forensic validity.
    2. The importance of full purchase history in an Amazon account to conduct an accurate forensic
       examination of the data.
           o A complete forensic review requires examining full purchase history, search logs,
               recommendation exposure, and product interactions.
           o Isolated purchases or searches without full historical context can lead to misleading
               conclusions.
           o Amazon’s data retention and account history logs are critical in determining user
               behavior trends, purchase frequency, and algorithmic influences.
    3. The role of Amazon’s Algorithm-Driven Recommendation System and Cross-Platform Tracking.
           o Amazon’s AI and machine learning (ML) systems do not passively reflect searches; they
               actively shape user experience and influence product visibility.
           o The Buy Box system prioritizes certain sellers and dynamically adjusts pricing based on
               AI predictions of consumer behavior.
           o The Clickstream tracking system records user interaction data, affecting what products
               are suggested or displayed.
           o AI-powered algorithms determine rankings, auto-complete searches, and personalize
               product placement.
           o Amazon’s data models can reinforce specific product categories, repeatedly exposing
               users to related items based on engagement rather than intent.
    4. Omnichannel shopping extends beyond a single device.
           o Amazon accounts are synchronized across multiple devices, including smartphones,
               tablets, desktops, laptops, and smart home devices (Alexa, Echo, etc.).
           o Shared household accounts introduce forensic challenges, as multiple individuals
               contribute to search history, purchases, and product recommendations.
           o Cross-device activity tracking allows Amazon to retain behavioral data across different
               platforms, affecting search results and purchase recommendations.

5. The Influence of Amazon’s Advertising System on Click and Purchase Behavior.
        o Amazon’s advertising ecosystem is deeply integrated into the search and
            recommendation process.
        o Sponsored listings, retargeting ads, and display ads shape user choices, often overriding
            organic search results.
        o Unlike a simple “search and buy” experience, Amazon actively pushes promoted content
            through AI-driven ad targeting.
        o Personalized recommendations are not neutral but based on bidding systems where
            advertisers pay to appear in a user’s feed.
6. Amazon’s Sponsored Products and Display Ads allow sellers to pay for visibility, impacting
    product exposure and forensic interpretation.
        o A product can appear in search results not because a user searched for it, but because an
            advertiser paid for placement.
        o The "Frequently Bought Together" and "Customers Also Bought" sections are influenced
            by paid sponsorships and algorithmic weighting, not purely organic trends.
        o To properly assess forensic relevance, all Amazon ad data should be reviewed, including:
            a) What sponsored products were displayed to this account?
            b) What retargeting ads followed the user across platforms based on past activity?
            c) Were certain product recommendations organic, or manipulated through paid
            placements?
            d) Did Amazon’s algorithm repeatedly push certain products based on prior engagement
            rather than active user searches?
7. Cart and Wishlist Data are an important part of forensic examination as it relates to Amazon.
        o The addition, removal, or modification of items in a cart or wishlist provides insight into
            purchasing intent.
        o Indecision and comparative shopping behaviors are common, and forensic analysis must
            account for all list interactions.
        o Amazon’s AI tracks wishlist and cart activity to refine recommendations and alter
            subsequent search results.
8. In his expert opinion, when evaluating the purchase or search of a product on Amazon as
    evidence, it is essential to ensure that it is presented in its full context rather than in isolation.
        o Forensic best practices require examining broader user engagement trends rather than
            isolated transactions.
        o A search or purchase cannot be evaluated without consideration of Amazon’s AI-driven
            content curation and advertising models.
        o Clickstream analysis, browsing history, and algorithmic reinforcement must be
            considered before attributing a specific intent to an individual search or purchase.
9. Many Amazon accounts, including the one in this case, are shared among family members,
    making it difficult to attribute a single purchase to one specific individual.
        o Amazon does not differentiate between users within a shared account.
        o Without analyzing full account activity logs, it is not possible to determine who
            conducted a specific search or made a purchase.
        o Device usage history, login timestamps, and behavioral tracking data are critical to
            accurate forensic conclusions.
10. Obtaining and reviewing Amazon data prior to March 2022 is critical to the ability to draw
    conclusions or put context into any purchasing.

•   Pre-March 2022 purchase and search data is necessary to establish historical buying patterns and
    determine whether a transaction was an anomaly or part of a long-term behavior trend.
