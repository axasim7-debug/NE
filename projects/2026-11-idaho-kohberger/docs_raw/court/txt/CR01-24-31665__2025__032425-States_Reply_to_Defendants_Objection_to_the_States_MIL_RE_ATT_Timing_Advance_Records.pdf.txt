        COMES NOW the State of Idaho, by and through the Latah County Prosecuting Attorney,

and respectfully replies to the Defendant’s “Objection to the State’s Motion in Limine RE: AT&T

Timing Advance Records” filed on March 17, 2025.

        Timing Advance Records refer to data that is used by providers to estimate the location of a

mobile device. The collection of this data is necessary for a cellular network to work properly.

Carriers do not retain this data indefinitely.




STATE’S REPLY TO OBJECTION TO THE STATE’S MOTION IN LIMINE RE: AT&T TIMING ADVANCE
RECORDS                                                                                                    1
       Regarding AT&T, before June 2023, AT&T retained timing advance records for seven (7)

days. These records were not routinely provided in response to legal demand prior to June 2023.

After June 2023, AT&T started providing these records pursuant to legal demand and began

retaining timing advance records for thirteen (13) months.

       In this case, the homicides occurred on November 13, 2022. Bryan Kohberger was

identified as a suspect December 19, 2022. Search warrants for his AT&T records were executed on

December 23, 2022. At this point, timing advance records were not available for Bryan Kohberger’s

phone to cover the relevant time period - November 13, 2022. Despite Defendant’s repeated

assertions, the fact of the matter is that AT&T did not provide Timing Advance Records to law

enforcement for Bryan Kohberger’s phone. The State is attaching Affidavits from AT&T verifying

that the State did not receive timing advance records for Bryan Kohberger’s phone. See State’s

Exhibit S-1 and Exhibit S-2.

       Based on the above, statements or inferences by defense counsel or their witnesses that there

is additional evidence that could have been provided, or was not provided, is simply not correct and

should not be allowed. Under I.R.E. 403 any probative value of such evidence is outweighed by the

danger of misleading the jury, undue delay, and waste of time. For these reasons, the State requests

the Court issue an order in limine prohibiting the defense from making any reference to the absence

of AT&T Timing Advance Records for Bryan Kohberger.
