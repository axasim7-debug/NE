Field Notes:
On 11/16/22 at approximately 1912 PST, Forensic Scientists (FS) Kerry Hogan, Hailey Youngling, Jennie
Ayers, Wyatt Barie and I arrived at the Idaho State Police District 2 (ISP D2) office located at 2700 Old N
S Hwy, Lewiston, Idaho 83501, to assist with a homicide investigation. Detective Talbott (ISP D2) was
present upon our arrival. Detective Talbott briefed us on the scene information.


1122 King Rd.

On 11/17/22 at approximately 0854 PST, FS Hogan, Youngling, Ayers, Barie and I arrived at 1122 King Rd,
Moscow, Idaho 83843. Detective Talbott was present upon our arrival. 1122 King Rd is a white colored
three story home that faces north in a residential neighborhood. The house consists of 6 bedrooms and
3 bathrooms. The first floor consists of a west bedroom, a bathroom, a furnace room, an east bedroom
and a stairway to the second floor. The second floor consists of a living room, a bathroom, a west
bedroom, an east bedroom, a kitchen, a storage room under the stairs and a switchback staircase to the
third floor. The third floor consists of a west bedroom, a landing, a bathroom and an east bedroom.

The second floor west bedroom had few belongings. A bed was against the south wall and a table with
two drawers was against the north wall. A freestanding mirror and furry chair were in the northwest
corner of the room. There were a number of apparent bloodstains on the bed, the south wall, the west
wall, the door and the floor.

In the third floor east bedroom a bed was against the north wall and a closet was against the south wall.
A desk was against the west wall. There were a number of apparent bloodstains on the bed, the north
wall, the east wall, the west wall and the door.

FS Barie and Ayers took photographs of the scene using the LizQ Imaging System.

 DEFINITION OF CONCLUSIONS
 Identification
 An Identification is established when the latent print examiner (Latent Print Analyst ( LPA) or Latent Print Supervisor (LPS) determines that there are
 sufficient features in agreement to conclude that two areas of friction ridge impression originated from the same source. Source refers to the area of
 friction skin.

 Exclusionon (i.e. impressionon was not made by)

 Exclusion is the conclusion made by the latent print examiner (LPA or LPS) that there are sufficient features in disagreement to conclude that two
 areas of friction ridge impression did not originate from the same source. Exclusion of a subject can only be reached if all needed comparable areas
 are represented and legible in the known exemplars.

 Inconclusive
 An Inconclusive conclusion occurs when:

               a)   The latent print examiner (LPA or LPS) ts unable to identify or exclude due to an absence of complete and legible known prints. In
                    such an instance, the inconclusive conclusion means that the impression needs to be re-examined using clearly and completely
                    recorded known impressions.

               6)   The latent print examiner (LPA or LPS) observes corresponding features but not sufficient to identify. Likewise dissimilar features
                    may be observed but not sufficient to exclude. In either case, the inconclusive conclusion means that the unknown impression was
                    neither identified nor excluded as originating from the same source.

 No Value (for comparison         purposes}

 A No Value decision is established wh len the latent print examiner (LPA or LPS) determines that the area of friction ridge impression lacks sufficient
 clear ridge detail that no determination for identification.nor exclusion is possible.

 Of Value (for comparison purposes
 A latent impression is "of value" when there is enough data present to allow a meaningful comparison to known exemplars with the possibility that a
 conclusion can be reached.

 AUTOMATED LATENT PRINT SYSTEM (ALPS) DATABASE SEARCHES

 When the Iatent examination report states that a latent print was entered into a database. the extent of database search may be as follows:

               State: State level search of the California Department of Justice Automated Latent Print System database.

               Local: (Optional): Other local databases available for the California Department of Justice to search, such as the Western Identification
               Network (WIN), San Bernardino/Riverside County databases, etc.

               FBI: National level search of the Federal Bureau of Investigation's Next Generation Identification (FBI/NGI) Automated Fingerprint
               Identification System (AFIS) database.

 Conducive (for an automated search)
 A friction ridge impression that is Conducive contains a sufficient number of ridge characteristics (minutia) within the area generally captured during
 feature extraction to provide a reasonable expectation of accurate search results if searched through an Automated Fingerprint Identification System
 (AFIS).
 Non Conducive (for an automated search)

 Due to a lack of sufficient minutia, friction ridge clarity or the area of the friction ridge skin recorded, it would not be reasonable to expect accurate
 search results from an automated search of a Not Conducive friction ridge impression. It is possible to have an impression that is identifiable but not
 conducive for an AFIS search.

 Registration
 Latent impressions that have been searched in the State, Local or FBI NGI databases and remain unidentified may be registered in the State's
 Unsolved Latent Database (ULDB) and/or the FBI NGI's Unsolved Latent File (ULF), which are continuously searched against new incoming
 records. Unsolved latent prints will be maintained in the State and FBI NGI databases until removed based on the crime type or the retention policy.

Field Notes:

On 12/30/22 at approximately 0705 PST, Forensic Scientists (FS) Hailey Youngling, Jennie Ayers and I
responded to 1630 NE Valley Rd, #G201, Pullman, Washington to assist in a homicide investigation.
Jeffory Talbott with Idaho State Police District 2 (ISP D2) and Dustin Blaker with Moscow Police
Department arrived to the residence with us. Deputy Chief Dawn Daniels with Washington State
University Police Department (WSU PD) was present upon our arrival. The scene was not secured at this
time. The temperature was approximately 33 degrees Fahrenheit and the weather was snowy and wet.

1630 NE Valley Rd #G201 is a beige six-plex apartment building with a maroon/brown colored “G” on
the exterior. Apartment #G201 is on the second floor on the east side of the building. The second floor
staircase is flush with the surrounding sidewalk and has a single flight of stairs going up. The entryway
to apartment #G201 is directly east of the staircase.

The interior of apartment #G201 was well kept and sparsely furnished. There are two bedrooms, one
bathroom with a separate water closet, a living room and a kitchen. The entryway door enters to the
living room. The living room was furnished with a two-seat sofa on the east wall. There was a coffee
table and a rug in front of the sofa. On the west wall, there was a television stand with a television and
various books on the interior shelving. In the southwest corner of the living room, there was a
disconnected air conditioning type unit. The south wall of the living room has a door that opens to the
exterior patio. To the north of the television stand, there was a table with one lamp, powered off. On
the north wall of the living room, there is a closet. East of the closet (on the north side of the
apartment) are two bedrooms, one on the west side, one on the east side. The northwest bedroom was
unfurnished and was locked prior to entry. There are two empty 2-door closets located on the west
