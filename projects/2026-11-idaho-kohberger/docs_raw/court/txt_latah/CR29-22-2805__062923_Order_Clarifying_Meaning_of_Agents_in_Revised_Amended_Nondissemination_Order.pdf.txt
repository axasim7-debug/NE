        On June 23, 2023, the Court issued a Revised Amended Nondissemination Order (“Revised

Order”). Accompanying the Revised Order were two lengthy opinions: Order Denying Shanon

Gray 's Request t0 be Exempt ﬁom the Amended Nondissemination Order and Granting Request that

the Order be Clarified and Order Denying the Associated Press ’s Motion to Vacate the Amended

Nondissemination Order. In this Court’s view, those opinions made clear that “agents” of attorneys

involved in the case, including law enforcement, could have, and would have, their speech regulated

under the Revised Amended Nondissemination Order.‘ However, following the issuance of those

decisions and the Revised Order, the Court took judicial notice of some news articles stating that law

enforcement were not longer bound by the Revised Order.

        In an effort to clarify that misunderstanding, this Court issues the following guidance.

        The Revised Order applies to: “The prosecuting attorneys, defense attorneys, any agents of

the prosecuting attorneys and defense attorneys, and any attorneys representing witnesses, victims, or

a victim’s family[.]” These individuals are not completely barred from speaking about the case but

must adhere to the restrictions on their speech imposed by the Revised Order.

        “Agent” is deﬁned as “someone who is authorized to act for or in place of another; a

representative.” AGENT, Black's Law Dictionary (l 1th ed. 2019). “Agents” of the prosecuting

attorneys include law enforcement working on this case or privy to conﬁdential information

involving the case. Similarly, investigators hired by the defense are “agents” of the defense. This is

because those individuals are working at the direction of the attorneys to investigate the case,

interview witnesses, and gather evidence. Stated differently, law enforcement and defense

investigators are acting as representatives of the attorneys. The attorneys have the authority to direct

certain actions of those individuals when it comes to building their cases for trial. Other “agents”

include staff in the prosecutor’s and defense attorneys’ ofﬁces and expert witnesses.

        This Order does not change anything in the Revised Order, but merely helps to further clarify

who the Revised Order applies to.
