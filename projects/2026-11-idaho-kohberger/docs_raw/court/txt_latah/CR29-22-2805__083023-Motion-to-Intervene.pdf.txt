         On June 23, 2023, this Court entered an order allowing 20 media outlets, with the

Associated Press as the lead media outlet, to intervene in this case for the limited purpose of

challenging the then-in-place Amended Nondissemination Order. Order Granting Motion to

Intervene for Limited Purpose, at 1-2. A nearly identical media coalition, at this time consisting

of 13 outlets,1 outlets, again seeks to intervene, this time for the limited purpose of opposing

Bryan C. Kohberger’s Motion to Remove Cameras from the Courtroom (“Motion”), filed August

24, 2023, and currently set for hearing on September 1, 2023, and for any other proceedings

affecting media access to the proceedings, including whether cameras will be allowed in the

courtroom at trial.

         The Idaho Supreme Court in The Associated Press v. Second Judicial District, 529 P.3d

1259 (Idaho 2023) made clear that even absent a specific rule in Idaho, intervention by the media

on a limited basis is appropriate to defend the public’s right to access and free speech. Id. at

1267. Here, intervention is particularly appropriate where the Court previously sought input

         Mr. Kohberger asks this Court to Remove Cameras in the Courtroom on the grounds that

the media have failed to heed the Court’s admonishment “not to focus strictly on Mr. Kohberger

publications do not support his position, however. For example, Mr. Kohberger cites to a Fox

News article that describes the Court’s statement to photographers and camera persons that they

should not focus exclusively on Mr. Kohberger. That Fox News story does not focus strictly on

Mr. Kohberger, however. It includes photos of: (a) Mr. Kohberger and defense counsel Jay

Logsdon entering the courtroom; (b) defense counsel Anne Taylor and Elissa Massoth entering

the courtroom; (c) Latah County Prosecuting Attorney Bill Thompson in the courtroom; (d) the

murder victims; (e) Mr. Kohberger entering the courtroom; and (f) a still photo from video of an

Indiana law enforcement stop of the vehicle Mr. Kohberger and his father were in on December

15, 2022. See Exhibit A, attached.

         Finally, many of the outlets who seek to intervene have significant experience

broadcasting live courtroom proceedings, including in high profile trials that resulted in both

acquittals (Wisconsin v. Kyle Rittenhouse, Ohio v. Skyler Richardson) and convictions (South

Carolina v. Alex Murdaugh). Their camera equipment and operators are experienced and not the

large, clunky, numerous camera equipment and operators that the Supreme Court expressed

concern about in Estes v. State of Texas, 381 U.S. 532 (1965). In Estes, there were at least 12

camera operators in the courtroom for a pretrial hearing, and the equipment journalists used was

noisy, required cables lying throughout the courtroom, and, at trial, involved some newsreel-style

cameras. Id. at 536. The Court can take judicial notice that similar equipment is not involved

here. Plainly, the Supreme Court’s concerns in Estes do not require removal of the more modest,

less intrusive camera equipment being used by reporters in this case.

         Accordingly, the media coalition seeks to intervene for the limited purpose of opposing

Mr. Kohberger’s motion to remove cameras from the courtroom and to make full argument and

briefing on this issue.
