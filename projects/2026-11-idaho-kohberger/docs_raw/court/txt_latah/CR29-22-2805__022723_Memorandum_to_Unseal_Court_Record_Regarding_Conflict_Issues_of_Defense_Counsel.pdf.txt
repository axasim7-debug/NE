       Idaho Court Administrative Rule 32 governs the release, exemption from release and
 3
       limitations upon release of judicial records. Specifically, I.C.A.R. 32 (i) outlines the
 4

 5     procedure for sealing court records:

 6     In ruling on whether specific records should be disclosed, redacted or sealed by order of

 7     The court, the court shall determine and make a finding of fact as to whether the interest

 8     in privacy or public disclosure predominates. If the court redacts or seals records to

 9     protect predominating privacy interests, it must fashion the least restrictive exception
10     from disclosure consistent with privacy interests. Before a court may enter an order
11     redacting or sealing records, it must also make one or more of the following
12
       determinations in writing:
13
               (1) That the documents or materials contain highly intimate facts or statements,
14                 the publication of which would be highly objectionable to a reasonable
                   person, or
15
               (2) That the documents or materials contain facts or statements that the court
16                 finds might be libelous, or
17             (3) That the documents or materials contain facts or statements, the
18                 dissemination or publication of which would reasonably result in economic
                   or financial loss or harm to a person having an interest in the documents or
19                 materials, or compromise the security of personnel, records or public
                   property of or used by the judicial department, or
20
               (4) That the documents or materials contain facts or statements that might
21                 threaten or endanger the life or safety of individuals, or
22
               (5) That it is necessary to temporarily seal or redact the documents or materials
23                 to preserve the right to a fair trial.

24
       State v. Allen, 156 Idaho 332, 336, 325 P.3d 673, 677 (Ct. App. 2014). That Rule
25
       provides: “The public has. A right to examine and copy the judicial department’s

               Since the record has been sealed regarding the conflict issues regarding Defense
11

15             It is also my understanding that there were no witnesses called during the hearing and

17   Taylor.

               In determining whether to seal the record of the hearing on the conflict issues of
19
     Defense Counsel, Ann Taylor, the Court, would have to analyze and apply I.C.A.R. 32 (i) and
20
     public policy case law.
21

22             Conflict issues in this case would involve Ms. Taylor’s ability to represent Mr.

23   Kohberger after previously representing Cara Kernodle (the mother of one of the Victims’ in

24   this case Xana Kernodle) as well as any other actual or potential conflicts with any other
25   clients. In addition, the court would have had to address any issues regarding why Ms. Taylor
26
     withdrew from Cara Kernoodle’s representation as a current client in order to take on Mr.

     Kohberger’s case. That would include if there has been a waiver filed by Cara Kernoodle
 2

 3   and/or possibly eliciting testimony from Cara Kernoodle to determine the extent of any contact

 4   and information relayed to Ms. Taylor during the representation. Additionally, the Court

 5   should have considered the potential for a conflict to arise at different stages of the criminal
 6   proceedings based on Mrs. Taylor’s prior representation of any witnesses or parties involved in
 7

             Upon gathering this information, the Court should have first determined whether the
 9
     sealing the record of the conflict hearing is consistent with openness and public policy.
10

11   Secondly, the Court should have determined whether sealing the record of the conflict hearing

12   is consistent with the type of hearing being “historically been open to the press and general

13   public”. Finally, the Court would need to have applied I.C.A.R. 32 (i) and made a finding of
14
     fact regarding the applicable issue and fashion the least restrictive exception, consistent with
15
     privacy interests.
16
             Taking into consideration the information the court reviewed in this case which was
17
     merely, Defense Counsel, Ann Taylor’s testimony to the court, the scope of the information
18

19   obtained by the Court from Ms. Taylor, and the lack of any findings on this matter, the Court

20   must unseal the court record regarding the conflict issues with Defense Counsel.
21           In applying the above considerations, it is not common to seal records regarding
22
     conflict issues of attorneys. At this stage of the criminal proceeding conflict issues should be
23
     open to the public. This avoids any actual or potential conflicts the court may not be aware of
24
     and helps insure any actual or potential conflicts may be avoided. By opening the record to the
25

26   public the Court can fully analyze all conflicts not just those recognized by Defense

 2   other conflicts regarding Defense Counsel should be thoroughly investigated.

             In applying, I.C.A.R. 32 (i), the Court must have made a determination that that the
 4
     record of the Conflict issues contained one or more of the following:
 5
             1) Highly intimate facts or statements the publication of would be highly
 6              objectionable to a reasonable person, or
 7           2) The record contains facts or statements that the Court finds might be libelous, or
             3) The record contains facts of statements that may compromise financial security or
 8              economic or financial loss to a party having an interest in the proceeding, or
             4) The record contains facts or statements that might threaten or endanger the life or

             6) The record contains personal data identifiers that should have been redacted.
