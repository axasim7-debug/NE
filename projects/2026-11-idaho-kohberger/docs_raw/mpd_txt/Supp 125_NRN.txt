3.        Narrative

On 11/13/2022, at approximately 1205 hours, I arrived at 1122 King Rd. reference
an unconscious subject. It was quickly determined the scene was likely a
quadruple homicide based on our observations. When I arrived on the third floor
to assess the scene, I noticed a medium sized curly haired tan dog on the bed
inside the western third floor bedroom (later identified as Kaylee Goncalves'
room). The dog appeared timid/scared and didn't react to our presence. Due to
the chaos surrounding the scene, my initial concern was preserving the scene. As
part of that process, I asked one of the officers to shut the door, so the dog
did not attempt to jump on the bed with any of the victims.
After the area was cordoned off and the area was secure, Cpl. Snyder began
taking photos the scene. At some point prior to this process, the dog moved from
the bed to under the desk in Kaylee's room.
After photos were completed I asked LCSO Deputy Olson to respond and assist by
taking the dog to the Humane Society of the Palouse for safekeeping. I collected
the dog from under the desk. The dog was timid and took some coaxing to move
from under the desk, but did not appear to have any obvious injuries or stains
on it's body or paws. Due to the amount of blood inside the bedrooms where the
victims were located and the lack of blood on or around the dog, I don't believe
the dog was on or around the victims. I walked the dog to the front door, where
Ofc. Warner was stationed, and asked him to hold onto the dog until Dep. Olson
arrived. A short time later, Dep. Olson arrived. I walked the dog from Ofc.
Warner to Dep. Olson, who was standing in the street. At my request, Dep. Olson
transported the dog to the Humane Society of the Palouse. I asked Dep. Olson to
write on the intake sheet for the Humane Society to not release the dog to
anyone unless the MPD gave approval first.

After I released the dog to Dep. Olson, I resumed managing the scene until I was
relieved by the Detective Unit.

4.     Other
I did not take any photos of the dog specifically during my response to the
scene. Some of the photos taken by Cpl. Snyder (evidence Item #1) have the dog

In reviewing Ofc. Nunes BWC of his initial response and clear of the residence,
