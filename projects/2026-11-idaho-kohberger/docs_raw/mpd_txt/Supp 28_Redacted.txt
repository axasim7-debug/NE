On November 18, 2022, I again attempted to view the contents of the SDCard, I
plugged the card into a write-blocker and it still was not recognized by the
computer. I used FTK Imager version# 4.7.1.2 to make an image of the SDCard. Now
that the data was on a drive recognized by the operating system, I then used
Axiom Magnet version# 6.5.0.32778 to parse data from the card.
In reviewing the data, I found over 13,000 individual images from the card. They
appear to be individual video frames from the camera.
While attempting to view the data on the card, I received a forwarded email from
C       N   , whose sister M     K    resides at the residence. The email stated
that he was viewing the video remotely when Ofc Morris removed the camera. He
stated he saw a suspicious vehicle in the area and was concerned with the video
footage stored on the device being overwritten.
I was then put in touch with T      L   , another resident at      King Road.
L    stated she would be back in Moscow at approximately 2000 hours. I met with
L    at her residence and asked again if she would be willing to assist in
recording screen captures on her phone from the device. I explained to L     I
would replace the camera, gather the information and then take the camera as
evidence. I also explained to L    I needed to take the wireless router, since
the camera was already linked to the WIFI network. L    stated she would consent
and allow both items to be seized.
I reinstalled the camera in the light socket and it powered on. I was able to
watch as L    accessed the footage on the camera. During this time, the camera
showed a vehicle entering the area at approximately 0356 hours and leaving at a
high-rate of speed at approximately 0420 hours. L    provided the videos to me.
I uninstalled the camera and seized the wireless router. Both items were then
taken back to the Moscow Police Department as evidence.
On November 19, 2022, while reviewing the videos located at 0356 and 0420, it
appeared the vehicle left the area and may have travelled on Walenta Drive,
however, the video stopped. In an attempt to see further, and gain perhaps a
better resolution, I reconstructed the wireless network the camera operated on
by powering on the wireless router. I downloaded the Homeye Pro app on my work
phone.

I was able to use the app and download the video clips. These clips included
audio, which were not a part of the screenshots completed and were able to
capture a longer duration. In the clips, the vehicle's exhaust and acceleration
can be heard, as well as seeing the vehicle leave at a high rate of speed on
Walenta Drive.

After capturing the clips, I disconnected the camera and wifi router and placed
them into evidence. The videos were placed onto a DVD and placed into evidence.
