3.        Narrative

On July 2, 2024 I accessed the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration's
website for historical weather data titled National Centers for Environmental
Information. Within this website I accessed the local climatological data tool
and selected the state of Washington to search for weather stations. I located
the Pullman Moscow Regional Airport weather station in the search results and
requested data for the dates of 11/12/22 to 11/13/22.
Within this tower data I selected the following information: Daily Output,
Hourly Output, Hourly Precipitation Output, Hourly Remarks Output, and
Documentation. I completed the above-mentioned date range and requested the data
be sent to my department email.

Later that same day I received an email saying the data was prepared for
download. I downloaded the file onto my department laptop and attached it to
this case file electronically. The PDF document provided was nineteen (19) pages
in length. Upon review of the document and the provided information key at the
bottom I was able to determine what portions of the report were reporting on the
requested dates.
The most pertinent information was located on pages two and three. These pages
were a report on Local Climatological Data Hourly Observations. The hours from
1433 hours on 11/12/22 until 0653 hours on 11/13/22 show the observation of
"OVC:08" under sky conditions. Each entry also has a single number after "OVC:
08". On page ten of the document is a section detailing what these values mean.
The following is from the document:
 "Sky Conditions: A report of each cloud layer (up to 3) giving the following
information. Each layer given in the following format: ccc:ll-xxx where: 1) ccc
is Coverage: CLR (clear sky), FEW (few clouds), SCT (scattered clouds), BKN
(broken clouds), OVC (overcast), VV (obscured sky), 10 (partially obscured sky).
2) ll is Layer amount used in conjunction with coverage code above. Given in
eighths (aka "oktas") of sky covered by cloud. Specifically 00-08 indicates the
number of oktas that cloud layer takes up in the total sky. 00 corresponds to
CLR, 01-02 corresponds to FEW, 03-04 corresponds to SCT, 05-07 corresponds to
BKN and 08 corresponds to OVC. 09 indicates an obscuration (i.e. the sky cannot
be seen due to obscuring phenomena - e.g. due to smoke, fog, etc.). 10 indicates
a portion of the sky is obscured (i.e. partial obscuration). For additional
information see Integrated Surface Data documentation.
(http://www1.ncdc.noaa.gov/pub/data/ish/ish-format document.pdf) in Cloud and
Solar Data portion of Additional Data Section."
This information appears to show the sky was overcast for the requested time
period.
